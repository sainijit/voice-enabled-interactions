1. Core vocabulary they expect

The second slide is the clearest definition:

Term	Starts	Stops	What it represents
Voice-to-voice latency	Customer's last word	First sound at speaker	The complete latency the customer actually experiences
Endpointing delay	Customer's last word	Turn-end decision	How long the system waits to decide the customer has finished
Processing latency	Turn-end decision	First sound at speaker	Everything after the system decides the turn is complete
Transcription latency	Speech in	Transcript out	ASR processing time
LLM time to first token (TTFT)	Prompt in	First token out	LLM generation latency
TTS time to first byte (TTFB)	Text in	First audio byte	TTS audio-generation latency
The most important distinction

They are explicitly separating:

Customer's last word → turn-end decision

from:

Turn-end decision → first sound

So:

Voice-to-voice latency = Endpointing delay + Processing latency

For the prototype shown:

Endpointing delay = 593 ms median
Processing latency = 194 ms median
Voice-to-voice = 775 ms median

The numbers don't add perfectly because of measurement/rounding details, but that's the conceptual decomposition they're asking everyone to use.

2. What they want to call the main KPI

The slide strongly suggests that the primary KPI should be:

Voice-to-voice latency

Measured:

Customer's last word → first sound at the speaker

Not:

turn-end decision → first audio

And not:

LLM request → first token

And not:

TTS request → first byte.

This is because voice-to-voice is what the customer actually feels at the kiosk.

They explicitly propose:

“Retire ‘time to first audio’ as a KPI name.”

because different teams/vendors may be using that phrase to mean different things.

3. The three KPI levels they are effectively asking you to track

From the slides, I'd structure the kiosk metrics like this:

KPI 1 — Transcription latency

Speech → transcript

Prototype:

115 ms/call

Target shown:

< 200 ms

KPI 2 — Processing latency

Turn-end decision → first sound

Prototype:

194 ms median

This consists roughly of things such as:

Turn-end decision
       ↓
LLM TTFT
       ↓
LLM generation / response handling
       ↓
TTS TTFB
       ↓
audio/playback
       ↓
First sound at speaker

The slide specifically calls out:

LLM TTFT = 88 ms
TTS TTFB ≈ 80 ms for a 3-word opener
Processing latency = 194 ms median
KPI 3 — Voice-to-voice latency

Customer's last word → first sound at speaker

Prototype:

775 ms median
1,368 ms p95

This is the customer-facing KPI.

4. The vocabulary around endpointing is especially important

They are basically saying your system shouldn't immediately assume:

“ASR has produced a transcript, therefore customer has finished.”

Instead, you need an end-of-turn / turn-end decision.

The terminology they use is:

Endpointing delay

The quiet period between:

customer's last word

and

system deciding the customer has finished.

Their prototype currently has:

593 ms median endpointing delay

And they explicitly say this is not primarily a hardware limitation.

That's a very important distinction for your kiosk work.

5. Their proposed human-conversation terminology

The first slide introduces three ranges:

Human gap

0–200 ms

They describe this as the natural conversational gap.

They cite typical human conversational response timing around:

110–200 ms median

Kiosk target — typical

300–600 ms

This is the range they say partners are sizing the use case around.

They also reference:

300 ms — kiosk partner feedback/stretch target

and:

500 ms — Epic's voice-to-voice interpretation

Reads as hesitation

>700 ms

The slide says a gap past roughly 700 ms starts to feel like hesitation.

Reads as a machine

>1,000 ms

Above approximately 1 second, it starts feeling machine-like.

And the slide says:

failure past ~4 s

So their conceptual customer-experience scale is approximately:

0          200       300       600       700       1000
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
 Human       Kiosk target       Hesitation Machine
 gap         typical
